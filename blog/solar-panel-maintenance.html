Solar Panel Maintenance

Cleaning, checks and the simple habits that keep your panels performing well in Lahore's dust and summer heat — a practical maintenance guide.

Solar Maintenance · Published 24 November 2026 · Solar Installation Bahria Town Lahore

On This Page

  2. Cleaning Your Panels

  4. When to Call a Professional

Why Maintenance Matters

Solar panels have no moving parts, so they need very little maintenance compared with most things in a home. But "very little" is not "none". Dust, bird droppings and pollution build up on the glass, and even a thin layer blocks sunlight and reduces output. In Lahore, where dust is a fact of life for much of the year, cleaning makes a real difference.

The good news is that basic maintenance is simple, cheap and quick. A few habits, done regularly, keep your system performing as it should.

Cleaning Your Panels

Clean your panels with plain water and a soft cloth or sponge — early in the morning or in the evening, when the panels are cool. Never use harsh chemicals, abrasive pads or a high-pressure washer, all of which can damage the glass or the seals.

How often? It depends on your location and the season. In dusty periods, every few weeks is sensible; after rain, nature often does the job for you. If you notice output dropping and the panels look dirty, that is your cue. Some homeowners clean their own panels; others prefer to have it done professionally — either is fine, as long as it gets done.

What to Check Regularly

Every month or so, take a look at your system: are all the panels intact, with no cracks or visible damage? Is the mounting secure? Is there anything shading the panels that was not there before — a growing tree, a new structure next door? Check your inverter display or monitoring app too: is the system producing roughly what you expect for the season? A sudden, unexplained drop is worth investigating.

Also keep the area around your inverter and batteries clear and ventilated. Inverters generate heat and need airflow; batteries need the same.

When to Call a Professional

Call a professional if you see cracked panels, loose mounting, damaged wiring, water where it should not be, or an inverter showing a fault. Do not try to repair electrical components yourself — solar systems carry live DC voltage even when the grid is off, and that is dangerous without the right knowledge and tools.

An annual professional check is a sensible habit: mounting, wiring, inverter health and battery condition, all reviewed by someone who knows what to look for. If your system was installed by us, contact us and we will arrange it.

Safety First

Never climb onto a roof that is wet, steep or unstable to clean panels, and never work on a solar system while it is live. If your panels are hard to reach safely, hire someone with the right equipment. A clean panel is not worth a fall.

Maintenance is the unglamorous part of owning solar — but it is also the part that protects your investment. A little attention, a few times a year, is all it takes.

Keeping Simple Records

You do not need a spreadsheet to look after a solar system, but a few notes go a long way. Once a month, jot down what your system produced — from the inverter display or the monitoring app — and the date you last cleaned the panels. Over a few months you will learn what normal looks like for your home in each season.

That baseline is what lets you spot a problem early. If output drops sharply and the panels are clean, something has changed — shading, a connection, or the inverter — and it is worth a professional look. Without records, a slow decline can go unnoticed for a year. With them, you catch it in a month.

Keep your installer's number handy too. If your system was installed by us, contact us — a quick call about something that looks wrong is always better than waiting.
