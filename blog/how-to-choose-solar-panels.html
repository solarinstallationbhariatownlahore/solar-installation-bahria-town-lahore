How to Choose Solar Panels

Wattage is only part of the story. Efficiency, build quality, heat performance and warranty terms all matter — here is what to look for.

Wattage Is Not the Whole Story

When people compare solar panels, the first number they look at is usually the wattage — how much power a panel is rated to produce. Wattage matters, but it is only one part of the picture. A panel's real value is how much energy it produces over years on your roof, in your climate, and that depends on several other factors too.

Efficiency is one of them. A more efficient panel produces more power from the same roof area. If your roof is large, efficiency matters less; if roof space is tight, it matters a lot. Either way, efficiency should be weighed against price — the most efficient panel is not automatically the best value for your home.

Panel Types

Most residential panels today are monocrystalline — made from a single silicon crystal — which generally offers higher efficiency and a sleeker look. Polycrystalline panels, made from multiple silicon fragments, are usually cheaper but slightly less efficient. Both types work well; the choice depends on your roof space, your budget and what is available.

Beyond the cell type, look at the build: the frame, the glass, the junction box and the overall finish. A well-built panel survives Lahore's summers, monsoon rain and winter dust far better than a cheaply made one.

Heat, Dust and Lahore's Climate

Solar panels are tested at 25°C, but Lahore's rooftops get far hotter in summer — and panels lose a little efficiency as they heat up. The temperature coefficient on a panel's datasheet tells you how much output drops per degree above the test temperature. A lower (better) coefficient is worth having in our climate.

Dust is the other local factor. A layer of dust blocks sunlight and reduces output, which is why regular cleaning matters. Panels with good build quality and a sensible tilt shed dust and rain better than flat-mounted, poorly finished ones.

Warranties Worth Reading

Panels usually carry two warranties: a product warranty (against manufacturing defects) and a performance warranty (guaranteeing a percentage of output after a number of years). Read both. A long performance warranty means little if the manufacturer is unlikely to be around to honour it, so the reputation of the brand behind the panel matters too.

The Installer's Role

Here is the part brochures skip: even the best panel performs badly if it is installed badly — wrong angle, poor mounting, shading that was never checked, or wiring that loses power before it reaches your inverter. Panel choice and installation quality go together.

When we plan a system, we recommend panels that suit your roof, your budget and the local climate — and we explain why. If you want to talk through the options for your home, contact us for a free consultation. You can also read our guide to solar panel installation to see how the work is done.

Matching Panels to Your Roof

The best panel on paper is not the best panel for your roof if it does not fit well. Roof size decides how many panels you can place; roof direction and shading decide how much each panel will produce. A large, unshaded roof gives you freedom to choose on value. A small or partly shaded roof pushes you towards higher-efficiency panels, because every square metre has to work harder.

Mounting matters too. Panels need a secure structure that holds them at a sensible angle, lets air flow underneath to keep them cooler, and survives Lahore's summer heat and monsoon winds. During our assessment we measure the roof, check the structure and look at shading through the day — then recommend panels that fit the roof you actually have, not the roof in a brochure.
