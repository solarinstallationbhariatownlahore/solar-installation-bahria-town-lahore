Net Metering in Bahria Town Lahore

Net metering lets extra solar electricity flow back to the grid. Here is how it works for homes in Bahria Town Lahore.

Net metering is one of the most useful parts of going solar in Pakistan — and one of the least understood. In simple terms, it lets the extra electricity your solar panels generate flow back to the grid, instead of going to waste. This guide explains how net metering works for homes in Bahria Town Lahore, who it suits, and what the process looks like.

What Net Metering Is

Solar panels generate the most electricity in the middle of the day, when the sun is strongest. Many homes use less power at that hour than their panels produce — the family is out, the heavy appliances are off, and the surplus has nowhere to go. Net metering gives it somewhere to go: the electricity grid.

Under a net metering arrangement, the extra units your system generates are exported to the grid, and the grid supplies your home in the evening and at night, when the panels are not generating. Instead of the surplus being wasted, it is accounted for against the electricity you draw from the grid. The grid, in effect, acts as a store for your daytime surplus.

Lahore's long sunny days make this work well. A roof in Bahria Town with good sun exposure can generate far more in the middle of the day than the home uses at that hour — which is exactly the surplus net metering is designed to capture.

How It Works, Day to Day

On a normal day the pattern is simple. In the morning the panels start generating. Your home uses what it needs — the fridge, the fans, whatever is running — and the surplus flows out to the grid. In the evening, when the panels stop, your home draws power back from the grid as usual.

A bidirectional meter records both directions: the electricity you export and the electricity you import. That record is what the net metering arrangement is based on. Your system, your meter and your grid connection work together — which is why the system design and the metering arrangement are planned together, not separately.

If you have monitoring — an inverter display or a phone app — you can watch this happen: generation rising through the morning, export in the middle of the day, and import again in the evening. Over weeks and months, that picture tells you whether your system is sized the way it should be.

Who Net Metering Suits

Net metering suits homes whose panels produce more than the home uses during the day. That is common in Bahria Town, where roofs have good sun exposure and daytime loads are often modest. It works with on-grid systems and with hybrid systems — both are connected to the grid and can export surplus power.

It suits homes less well if the daytime load already uses everything the panels produce, or if the property has no reliable grid connection at all — in that case an off-grid system is the better choice. It is also worth knowing that net metering alone does not provide backup during an outage: when the grid goes down, a grid-connected system shuts down. Backup is what batteries are for, and it is a separate decision from net metering.

Whether net metering makes sense for your home is something we discuss during the consultation, once we know your load and your roof.

The Process, in Brief

The process starts with the system itself: a consultation, a load and roof assessment, and a system design. The net metering arrangement is discussed as part of that design, because the system size and the metering go together.

After installation, the net metering application and the meter arrangement follow. What the process involves and what is needed from you depends on your property and your electricity provider, so we go through it during the consultation rather than promising a fixed timeline here. What we can promise is that we explain each step before it happens, so there are no surprises.

What to Ask During Your Consultation

When you talk to us about net metering, these are the questions worth asking: does it apply to my property? What does the process involve, and what is needed from me? How should the system be sized so the arrangement works in my favour? And what happens to my backup power during an outage?
