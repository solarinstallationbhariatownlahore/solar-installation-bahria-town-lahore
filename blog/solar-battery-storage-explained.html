Solar Battery Storage Explained

What batteries actually do, what capacity means in real life, and how to think about backup without overspending.

Solar Batteries · Published 24 November 2026 · Solar Installation Bahria Town Lahore

What a Solar Battery Does

A solar battery stores the electricity your panels generate during the day so you can use it later — in the evening, at night, or during a power outage. Without a battery, daytime surplus is either exported to the grid (where net metering applies) or simply not used.

Batteries are a core part of hybrid and off-grid systems. They are not part of a basic on-grid system. Whether you need one depends on when you use power and how much backup matters to you.

Capacity: What the Numbers Mean

Battery capacity is measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh). One kWh is roughly enough to run a 100-watt light for ten hours — or, more usefully, to keep a few fans, lights and a fridge going for an evening. The number on the box is the total capacity; the usable capacity is usually less, because batteries should not be fully drained every time.

Depth of discharge is the term for how much of a battery's capacity you can safely use. Lithium batteries can typically be discharged much further than older lead-acid types, which is one reason they have become the standard for home solar storage.

Battery Types

Lithium batteries are the common choice for home solar today: they store more energy in less space, can be discharged further, last longer and need less maintenance. They cost more upfront.

Lead-acid batteries are cheaper to buy but bulkier, heavier, less efficient and shorter-lived, and they need more care. They still have a place in some off-grid setups, but for most homes in Bahria Town, lithium is the practical choice.

Sizing for Your Home

This is the decision that matters most — and the one where overspending is easiest. A battery sized to run your whole house through the night costs far more than one sized for essential loads: lights, fans, a fridge, phone charging and a few sockets.

We size batteries by asking what you actually want to keep running during an outage or in the evening, and for how long. Air conditioning, water heaters and other heavy loads change the answer completely, and we discuss that honestly rather than selling you the biggest battery available.

Lifespan and Replacement

Batteries do not last forever. Their lifespan depends on the type, how deeply they are discharged, and how hot they get. A lithium battery in a well-planned system can serve for many years, but it will eventually need replacement — a real cost that should be part of your decision from the start, not a surprise years later.

If you want to talk through battery options for your home, contact us for a free consultation. You can also read about our solar battery solutions and our hybrid vs on-grid guide.

Batteries and Load Shedding

For many families in Lahore, the real reason for buying batteries is load shedding. The question to ask is not "how big a battery can I buy" but "what do I need to keep running when the power goes out, and for how long?"

Make a list: lights in the rooms you use, ceiling fans, the fridge, phone charging, perhaps a television or a laptop. Add up what those draw, multiply by the hours you want them covered, and you have a realistic picture of the storage you need. Air conditioning changes the picture completely — a single air conditioner can use more in an hour than all your lights and fans combined — so be honest with yourself and your installer about whether cooling is part of your backup plan.

A battery sized to your real essential loads costs far less than one sized to run everything, and it will serve you better because it is used properly instead of being drained flat every night.
